Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 (Act 19) & 2019 Amendment

Traffic Law, Violation Penalties & Citizen Rights

Authoritative fine schedules, vehicle seizure regulations, driver rights during police stops, and procedure to dispute unfair e-Challans in Virtual Courts.

MV Amendment Act 2019 Rule 139 CMVR Compliant

🔑 Can Traffic Police Snatch Your Vehicle Keys?

Strictly Illegal

Under Indian Motor Vehicles Law and official Police Standing Orders, a police officer cannot forcibly snatch or remove keys from the ignition of a moving or stationary vehicle. Doing so constitutes arbitrary physical restraint. You have the right to politely request the officer's name, buckle number, and demand that they issue an official e-Challan rather than physically seizing keys.

🪪 Can Police Confiscate Your Physical Licence?

Only Under Section 206(4)

Under Section 206(4) of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, a physical licence can ONLY be impounded by an officer of Assistant Sub-Inspector (ASI) rank or above, and strictly for specified offences: drunk driving (Sec 185), dangerous driving (Sec 184), over-speeding (Sec 183), or carrying overload. The officer MUST issue a mandatory official temporary acknowledgment receipt on the spot.

📱 Are DigiLocker & mParivahan Documents Valid?

100% Legally Binding

Under Rule 139 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules (CMVR), 1989 and MoRTH Notification dated 17-12-2018, electronic documents produced through official DigiLocker or mParivahan apps carry the exact same legal status as original physical certificates. Police cannot compel physical production or issue a fine for not carrying physical cards.

🌬️ Breathalyzer Alcohol Testing Rules

Section 203 & 204 MVA

Police can request a breath test under Section 203. The citizen has the statutory right to request a fresh, sealed, sterile plastic pipe before blowing. If alcohol level exceeds 30 mg per 100 ml of blood (Section 185), police must take the driver for a medical blood test at a registered government hospital within 2 hours.

👩 Special Protections for Female Drivers

BNSS Statutory Safeguards

A female driver or passenger cannot be physically searched or bodily handled by a male police officer under any circumstances. Furthermore, under Section 43(5) of Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, female citizens cannot be detained or arrested between sunset and sunrise except under exceptional circumstances with prior Magistrate permission.

📸 Right to Record Video of Police Encounter

Citizen Constitutional Freedom

Recording a public official conducting duties in a public place is a protected constitutional freedom under Article 19(1)(a) of the Constitution of India, provided it does not physically obstruct the officer from performing lawful duties. Police officers cannot confiscate your mobile phone simply for filming a traffic stop.

Step-by-Step: How to Contest an Unfair e-Challan

1
Verify Photographic Evidence Online:
Visit the official Ministry portal at echallan.parivahan.gov.in. Enter your Challan number or Vehicle Registration number. Inspect the speed camera or CCTV snapshot to verify if your vehicle was wrongly identified or if number plate was misread.
2
Submit Grievance on Grievance Portal:
If camera captured an incorrect vehicle or cloned plate, click on "Grievance" on the Parivahan portal. Upload clear photographs of your vehicle's physical characteristics (make, model, color) contrasting with the challan snapshot.
3
Contest in Virtual Court (vcourts.gov.in):
When a challan is sent to the Judicial Magistrate Virtual Court, you have the option to:
  • Pleaded Guilty: Pay the compounded or mitigated fine amount directly through UPI/Netbanking.
  • Contest / Not Guilty: Submit written defense stating reasons (e.g. medical emergency, incorrect vehicle, signal malfunction). The case is referred to regular Metropolitan Magistrate Court for physical hearing.
4
Attend National Lok Adalat for Fine Reduction:
National Lok Adalats are held quarterly across District Court complexes and Traffic Headquarters. Pending traffic challans can be compromised or settled with substantial reductions (often 50% to 75% fine waiver) through amicable settlement.
